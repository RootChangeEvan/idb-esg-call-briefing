Briefing for the call with ESG leadership

Serge Troch (ESG Division Chief), David Maier (ESG Capacity Building Lead) and Julio. Prepared Oct 27, 2026 from the 12 documents Julio shared in the project folder.

The short version
  • The documents describe a risk-based, two-track system. ESG specialists work directly on the riskiest operations (Category A/B and high or substantial risk). Project Team Leaders carry everything else, with only advisory help from a small ESG Guidance Service. Perceptions of the ESPF are likely to differ sharply between these two tracks.
  • Most of the friction points the review should probe sit at hand-offs: screening at entry, risk-rating changes, the handover to execution, second-line reviews by RMG/ESR at ERM/QRR/OPC, and the coexistence of the old safeguard policies with the ESPF.
  • The procedures have been revised repeatedly since 2021 to "reflect reality" after restructuring. A new project-cycle regulation also took effect in March 2026. Ask what has changed and what is still settling.
  • The folder covers the rules well but says little about how they work in practice, such as data, reports or training records. The data-request list at the end targets that gap.

2. How ESG works, according to these documents

Who does what

  • Three lines of defense. VPS/ESG is the first line (designs E&S solutions in operations). RMG/ESR is the second line (independent quality assurance at ERM, QRR and OPC, plus portfolio monitoring). Audit, OVE and MICI form the third. Since March 2026, SPD/SQR is an additional second-line reviewer of project quality.
  • Inside ESG. Group Heads assign a Primary ESG Team Member to each supported operation, who leads the E&S documents and is the Project Team Leader's main contact. Other specialists join for resettlement, Indigenous peoples, biodiversity, labor and similar issues. Disagreements go to the Group Head, then the Unit Chief.
  • Sector divisions. Project Team Leaders in INE, SCL, IFD, CSD and INT are accountable for their operations and carry ESPF responsibilities alone wherever no ESG specialist is assigned.

The two tracks

  • With ESG specialists: Category A and B operations and high or substantial risk financial intermediaries in preparation, and all high or substantial risk operations in execution. At execution start these usually move to ESG staff in the regions.
  • With the Guidance Service only: Category C, low and moderate risk financial intermediaries, and policy-based loans without significant impacts. In execution, the PTL supervises and may ask the Guidance Service for advice only when specific triggers occur (design change, non-compliance, severe grievances, a fatality, other red flags).
  • Screening and rating run through a web Toolkit that produces the impact category, the risk rating and the disaster and climate risk classification. Ratings are revisited through the cycle, and upgrades to substantial or high go to ESG's operations leadership.

When things go wrong

  • Supervision reports carry a safeguard performance rating. A downgrade to Partially Unsatisfactory or Unsatisfactory triggers weekly checks, fortnightly updates and defined escalation meetings chaired by the ESG Division Chief, VPS or the Executive Vice President, with a menu of options that includes halting disbursements.
  • MICI cases follow tight, multi-level review chains with day limits for management responses.

Two policy regimes at once

The five earlier safeguard policies (OP-703, 704, 710, 761, 765) still govern operations approved or declared eligible before October 31, 2021. Staff may be working under both regimes in the same portfolio.

3. What this suggests for the review

These are observations from the documents, not findings. They're worth testing in interviews and focus groups.

  • Two populations with different experiences. Teams on high-risk operations work alongside ESG specialists. Teams on low and moderate risk operations, the majority in execution, carry ESPF responsibilities with advisory support only. The sample and the analysis should separate these groups so their views aren't averaged together.
  • Hand-offs are where perceptions likely form:
    • Toolkit screening at entry.
    • Risk-rating changes that need leadership sign-off.
    • The transfer from headquarters to regional ESG staff, or to the PTL, at execution start.
    • Repeated E&S review by RMG/ESR (and now SPD/SQR) at ERM, QRR and OPC, which staff may experience as duplication.
  • Policy versus support versus operations. Some likely complaints are really about structure and resources rather than the ESPF itself. Examples are the Guidance Service's limits (no non-objection, no drafting), workload-based assignment and rules changing after reorganization. The instruments should probe the source of each concern.
  • Change fatigue. Most protocols were revised in 2024 to "reflect reality" after roles were removed (HSO, SPL), and the project-cycle rules changed again in 2026. Questions about the ESPF may draw answers about reorganization.
  • Implied stakeholder groups to compare against IDB's list:
    • ESG Group Heads and Unit or Division leadership.
    • Primary ESG Team Members at headquarters and in the regions.
    • Guidance Service staff.
    • RMG/ESR.
    • PTLs on high-risk operations and PTLs on low or moderate risk operations.
    • Country office Chiefs of Operations.
    • SPD/SQR, LEG, and the grievance protocol coordinator.
